The ritual

Five grams, eighty degrees, ninety seconds.

Scroll, and the leaf does the rest — dry into the glass, water off the boil, then the slow turn from clear to gold that tells you it is ready.

01

Dry leaf

Five grams of whole leaf, unbroken. It should smell of chestnut before it ever meets water.

02

The pour

150 ml at 80 °C, poured down the side of the glass so the leaf is never scalded.

03

The steep

Ninety seconds. The leaf opens, sinks, and the water turns the colour of pale honey.

04

Ready

Chestnut, cut grass, a sweetness that arrives late. Four more steeps left in the leaf.

The pouch

Folded, stitched and stamped by hand.

No tins, no plastic tubs. Each pouch is folded from unbleached kraft, lined with a food-safe foil barrier, crimped along the top and stitched shut by the same four people in Mumbai who pack every order.

  • Foil lined, nitrogen flushedLight and oxygen are what kill green tea. The barrier holds a lot at pick-day freshness for eighteen months unopened.
  • Home compostable shellPeel the foil liner and the kraft outer breaks down in a garden bin in about twelve weeks.
  • Stamped with the lotGarden, altitude and pick date are hand-stamped on every pouch. No two lots carry the same stamp.
