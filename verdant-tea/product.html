Everything about this lot, in three questions

01Who

The Fang family, third generation

This lot comes from Fang Wei and her two brothers, who work eleven terraced hectares on the north face above Huangshan village. Their grandfather planted the oldest rows in 1961; those bushes still give the best leaf on the property.

We have bought their spring green every year since 2019. They pick it, they fire it in their own wok shed, and we take the entire spring lot — which is why it runs out.

02What

A pan-fired spring green, unoxidised

Picked as one bud and one leaf in the four hours between first light and full sun, then withered for ninety minutes and fired by hand in a dry wok at 140 °C to stop oxidation dead.

The result is flat, sword-shaped leaf that smells of chestnut dry and tastes of cut grass and sweet almond wet. It holds five steeps before it gives up.

03Why

Because altitude is a flavour you can taste

At 1,240 m the nights are cold and the cloud sits on the slope until mid-morning. The bush grows slowly, and slow growth concentrates amino acids — theanine above all, which is what makes a green tea sweet instead of merely grassy.

Blended supermarket green is pooled from dozens of gardens across a whole season, so that sweetness is averaged away. One garden, one week, one lot is the only way to keep it.

How to prepare it

Western style, in a glass. If you have a gaiwan, halve the water and the time, and expect eight steeps instead of five.

  1. 01

    Warm the glass

    Rinse with hot water and tip it out. Cold glass steals heat from the first steep and flattens the aroma.

    30 s
  2. 02

    Measure five grams

    Roughly two heaped teaspoons, but weigh it if you can — this leaf is flat and light, so spoons under-read by half.

    5 g / 150 ml
  3. 03

    Water at 80 °C

    Boil, then wait four minutes. Boiling water scorches unoxidised leaf and turns the sweetness into bitterness.

    80 °C
  4. 04

    Steep ninety seconds

    Pour down the side of the glass, not onto the leaf. Watch it sink — when the last leaf drops, it is ready.

    90 s
  5. 05

    Steep it again, four more times

    Add fifteen seconds each round. The third steep is the one most people call the best.

    ×5

Questions we actually get

How long does it keep once I open the pouch?

About four months at full quality. Roll the pouch down, press the air out and clip it — the foil liner does the rest. Do not decant it into a glass jar on a sunny shelf; light is what turns green tea flat and hay-like.

Is 80 °C really necessary, or is that tea snobbery?

It is necessary. Unoxidised leaf at 100 °C releases catechins fast, and those are the bitter, drying compounds. At 80 °C you get the theanine and the sugars first. If you have no thermometer, boil the kettle and wait four minutes with the lid off.

Why is there no harvest date on most tea I buy?

Because most tea is blended across gardens and seasons to hit a consistent price and taste, so a single date would be meaningless. That is a legitimate way to make tea. It is just not what we do.

What happens when this lot sells out?

It is gone. We take the whole spring picking from one family, and when the 210 kg is sold there is no more until the following April — and next April is a different tea, with a different stamp. Subscribers get a week's notice before each release.

Can I return it if I have already opened it?

Yes. Brew it properly, and if it is not right for you, email us within thirty days and we refund the full amount. You do not need to send the tea back — give it to someone who will drink it.
